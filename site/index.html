Inventory management is the easy part.

Every platform in this category is organized around the filing cabinet. Get the cars in, keep the fields tidy, push a list out the door. That is a spreadsheet with a login, and it is the part of your business that was never actually hard.

A car parked on your lot does not earn anything until somebody sees it. So Rooftop is organized around that instead — the shortest path between you took the car in and the car is in an ad somebody is looking at tonight. All the inventory tools everyone else has, sitting on top of a marketing engine instead of a filing system.

Lot Walk

Every move your lot makes, written down.

Lot Walk is the feed everybody watches. A unit landed. Recon closed in four days. Photos are up and it went front-line ready. Something crossed thirty days. Meta rejected a listing. Mike sold a truck. Your crew reads it the way they already read Facebook and comments on it the way they already text each other — nothing to learn, nothing to train.

It is also the record. Every post is a real event on a real VIN with a person and a timestamp on it, and it is kept. Most dealer software holds the current state of the car and overwrites the rest. Rooftop keeps the whole history, because the history is the only place the insight lives.

Questions

The ones every dealer asks.

How is this different from what I already pay for?

Most of it is built to hold your inventory and push a list out. Rooftop is built to keep you advertising. The difference shows up in small, specific places — a sold unit leaving your Meta catalog the same hour instead of on the next feed run, a rejection that tells you the image was too small instead of showing a red dot, a price cut you can trace to what it did to views three days later. Same inventory work, aimed at selling instead of storing.

Do I have to leave my DMS?

No. Rooftop sits next to it. Your DMS keeps the books, the deals, the titles and the floorplan. Rooftop handles the inventory record that goes out to the world — photos, merchandising, your website and every channel. Nothing gets ripped out.

Is the website really included?

Yes. It is in the monthly price, on your domain, and it runs off the same record as everything else — which is the whole point, because that is where your ad traffic lands. If you would rather keep the site you have, we can feed it instead; tell us who built it.

Do you run the ads too?

We can. Rooftop Auto is built by Litespeed Marketing, an advertising agency that has been buying media for twenty years and runs Meta campaigns for car lots right now. The platform stands on its own and plenty of dealers will run their own ads on it — if you would rather hand that part off, ask when we talk.

How fast does a price change actually reach each channel?

Your own site is instant. Meta usually re-crawls the catalog inside fifteen minutes. Google Vehicle Ads re-fetches the feed up to four times a day. Cars.com and Autotrader pull overnight, so an afternoon change is live by morning. We show you the real clock on every one rather than a checkmark that only means we sent it.

What if a listing gets rejected?

It shows up as a rejection with the actual reason — image too small, missing a required attribute, unmapped trim — on the unit and in your feed. A tool that catches a broken listing is worth more than one that pretends listings never break.

Can you bring my current inventory over?

Yes, and we do it, not you. Most lots are live in a few days. If your photos are already online we pull those too.

I run more than one lot. Does that cost double?

Founding pricing is $99 a month per rooftop. You can run them as separate storefronts or combine them into one website — a lot of groups do the second, because a shopper does not care which of your lots a truck is parked on.

What is the free trial, exactly?

Thirty days of the whole product, with no credit card. You make an account, we help you get your inventory in, and you use it. Nothing is switched off at the end without us talking to you first — there is no card on file to charge, so there is nothing to cancel and no way to be billed by accident.

One thing is held back until you are on a paid plan: registering a brand-new domain, because we pay the registrar for that one. If you already own your domain you can connect it on the trial, and that is what most lots do.

Am I signing a contract?

No. Month to month. The whole pitch is that this pays for itself in one extra unit turned, and if it does not, you should not be locked in.
